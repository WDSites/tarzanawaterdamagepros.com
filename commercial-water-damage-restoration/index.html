Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Tarzana, CA

A water loss in a shared building has more people involved. This page explains what we can do, who decides what and what to have ready.

Call (818) 337-2642

Commercial water damage restoration means drying and cleaning up a building that is not a single-family home. It might be an office suite, a retail unit, a shared hallway or a multi-unit building with common walls. The water work is the same in principle, but the number of people affected and the questions about who pays are different.

We handle the drying, cleanup and documentation side of these losses through the same 24/7 dispatch line used for houses. We do not rebuild, replace roofing or repair plumbing, and we say so up front. Very large or specialized losses may be beyond what a crew can take, and dispatch will tell you honestly.

Safety steps for a commercial space

Safety comes before cleanup. In a shared building, make sure these steps are covered by someone on site.

  • Move people out of rooms with standing water or sagging ceilings.
  • Shut off the water source if you can reach the valve, or have building maintenance do it.
  • Do not enter areas where water is touching outlets, panels or lighting, and call 911 for sparking or a gas smell.
  • Rope off wet floors and put up warning signs so no one slips.
  • Photograph the damage and note which units and areas are involved before anything is moved.

How the work is scoped

A crew starts with the building owner or manager and any tenants on site. The first step is to identify the source, the water category and the areas affected. Moisture readings and a thermal camera map what is wet, including ceilings and wall cavities that look dry.

Work is then planned around the space. Occupied suites may need containment so dust and equipment noise stay in the work area. After-hours coordination is something to ask dispatch about when you call. It is not a promise, and it depends on crews and on the building.

Drying, containment and odor control

Commercial finishes include suspended ceilings, carpet tile, glued-down flooring and metal studs. Each dries differently. Crews use air movers and dehumidifiers matched to the size of the space, and they remove wet insulation and ceiling tile that cannot be saved.

If the water was contaminated, affected porous material is removed and surfaces are cleaned. ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, describes the categories and drying classes that guide those decisions. For the equipment side of drying, see structural drying and dehumidification.

Owner, manager and tenant responsibilities

Who handles what depends on the lease and the building. Often the owner covers the structure and the tenant covers contents and improvements, but leases vary widely. Read yours, and ask the property manager who gives the go-ahead for emergency work.

We cannot decide that for you. We can explain the work, give a written scope and provide the records your insurer or manager asks for. Keep every receipt and every written notice you send or receive.

Insurance and records

The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, and flood coverage is sold separately through the National Flood Insurance Program. A plumbing failure and a flood are treated differently, so name the cause clearly when you report the loss.

Photos, moisture readings, equipment logs and a list of removed materials help a property insurer understand the loss. Report the damage promptly, keep damaged items until the adjuster approves disposal and read your policy for deadlines. We cannot promise any claim outcome.

What affects the cost of a commercial loss

Commercial jobs vary more than residential ones, so factors matter more than averages.

  • Size and layoutOpen floors, many small rooms and shared walls change how much equipment and containment are needed.
  • Water categoryClean water costs less to handle than contaminated water, which adds removal and cleaning steps.
  • MaterialsCeiling tile, carpet tile, drywall and flooring each have their own removal and drying needs.
  • OccupancyWorking around people and operations can add time and planning.
  • Rebuild scopeReconstruction is separate and is handled by the owner's contractor.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. For a number that fits your building, call the Tarzana line and describe the space.

Mistakes to avoid

Delay and guesswork cause most of the extra cost in building losses.

  • Waiting for approval to be settled before stopping the water.
  • Letting different parties move or discard materials without photos.
  • Assuming a dry ceiling tile means a dry plenum above it.
  • Hiring any contractor for any sizable job without checking the license on the Contractors State License Board site, which requires a license at that level.
