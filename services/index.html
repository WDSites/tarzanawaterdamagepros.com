Match the service to the situation

A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own service. Water comes in, someone stops it and pulls it out, the structure is dried, damaged material is removed and sanitized and the house is rebuilt. Mold can appear at any point if moisture lingers. Picking the right page first saves a phone call and often saves money.

Every service below is available through the same 24/7 dispatch line at the number on this page. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the written scope names what will be removed, dried or rebuilt.

The order most jobs follow

Most losses use more than one service. A burst supply line might start with extraction, move to drying and cleanup and finish with a rebuild. A roof leak might begin with drying and end with ceiling repair. A flood might add mold work weeks later if materials stayed wet.

Ask dispatch to describe the sequence for your loss, including which stage comes first and what will be decided at each step. A short plan is a good sign.

Standards and what they mean

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for professional mold remediation. They describe categories of water, drying classes, containment and documentation. It is reasonable to ask any company whether its crews work to them.

Insurance, permits and licensing are separate questions. Your insurer decides coverage, the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety answers permit questions and the Contractors State License Board lets you check a contractor's license.

Where services end

Restoration repairs damage after water gets in. It does not waterproof a foundation, fix a roof or replace plumbing, and we say so up front. If your problem is a repeating leak or a drainage issue, a plumber, roofer or drainage contractor is the right next call after the cleanup.
