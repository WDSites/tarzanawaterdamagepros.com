Crawl space water damage is the harm caused when water collects in the shallow space under a raised wood floor. It wets the soil, the insulation, the joists and the underside of the subfloor, and it can lift the humidity inside the house above it.

Our line is answered 24/7. A crew pumps out standing water, removes insulation that cannot be saved and dries the framing. We do not regrade a yard or install drainage, and we say which trade handles that part.

An honest note on how common crawl spaces are

Not every Tarzana home has one. Many houses sit on a concrete slab, and some have a raised foundation with a crawl space, especially older homes. If you are not sure which you have, look for small vents at the base of the exterior wall or an access hatch in a closet or hallway.

If your home is on a slab, the page on slab leak water damage is the better fit. If water is mainly in a garage or a lower room, see basement water damage cleanup.

How water gets under a raised floor

Water arrives from two directions, above and below. A supply line or drain under a floor can leak onto the soil, and a washing machine or tub overflow can run down through the subfloor. Runoff from a slope or a clogged drain can also flow in through foundation vents.

The Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety single-family general notes describe crawl space ventilation at 1 square foot per 150 square feet of under-floor area, with an access opening of 18 by 24 inches or a perimeter wall opening of 16 by 24 inches. Vents are meant to move air, and they can also let water in when a yard drains toward the house.

First-hour safety steps

A crawl space is a confined, low-clearance area with possible electrical and biological hazards. Be careful about what you do before help arrives.

  1. Do not enter the space if water is deep, if wiring is near it or if the soil is saturated.
  2. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the suspected source.
  3. Turn off power to any equipment under the house, such as a sump pump circuit that is wet, only from a dry panel.
  4. Keep children and pets away from the access hatch.
  5. Photograph what you can see from the opening and call the line.

Wires and standing water do not mix

If you see standing water around electrical boxes, cords or ducts, stay out and call. Never wade into a flooded crawl space to find the source.

Emergency: when crawl space water cannot wait

Water under the house is an emergency when it is rising, when it comes from a broken pipe or when the floor above has started to feel wet. Wood holds water, and subflooring that stays wet for several days begins to swell and soften.

A single damp patch with no source can wait for a morning call if the area is checked and the main valve is closed. When in doubt, call and describe what you see.

What the crew does

A crawl space job uses compact equipment and careful planning because the space is tight. Technicians work in protective gear and follow the order below.

  1. Pump out standing water with small submersible pumps and hoses routed outside.
  2. Remove wet insulation, loose debris and any damaged vapor barrier from the space.
  3. Take readings on joists, subfloor and sill plates with a pin meter.
  4. Set compact air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the framing and the soil surface.
  5. Return to re-read moisture before equipment is removed.

ANSI/IICRC S500 describes the drying approach. If the space shows signs of growth, that falls under mold remediation, a separate stage with its own containment.

Insurance and a wet crawl space

The California Department of Insurance says that if water damage indicates leakage over a period of time, mold may already have developed. Tell your insurer what you found and when, and ask about coverage for the cause.

Photograph the space before anything is removed, keep receipts and report the loss to your agent promptly. A crew can supply moisture readings for the file, and nobody can promise coverage.

Prevention for raised-foundation homes

Keep gutters and downspouts directing water away from the foundation, and keep foundation vents clear of soil and plants. Check for leaks under sinks and appliances, and look under the house after heavy rain.
