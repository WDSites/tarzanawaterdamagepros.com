Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Tarzana, CA

Carpet is a layered system, and the water that soaked it decides what stays and what goes.

Call (818) 337-2642

Wet carpet looks like one problem but behaves like four. There is the face fiber you walk on, the backing that holds it together, the pad underneath and the subfloor below that. Each layer holds water differently, and each dries at its own pace.

That is why the first question is not how wet the carpet is. It is what kind of water soaked it and how long it has been there. Those two answers decide whether a crew can dry it in place, lift and reinstall it or take it out for good.

Clean, gray or black water: what usually happens to carpet

On site, a crew sorts your loss into a water category using the source descriptions in ANSI/IICRC S500, a reference only.

Water sourceCategoryUsual outcome for carpet and pad
Supply line or faucetCategory 1, clean at firstCarpet may be dried in place if caught quickly. Pad is often replaced
Washer overflow or dishwasher leakCategory 1 or 2Carpet may be cleaned and dried, and pad is usually replaced
Toilet overflow with waste or a long-standing leakCategory 2 or 3Carpet and pad are usually removed
Sewage or storm runoffCategory 3Carpet and pad are removed. See sewage backup cleanup

Why the pad usually goes

Foam and rebond pad act like a sponge, and they hold water against the subfloor long after the surface feels dry. Pad is also cheap compared with the problems that wet pad can cause, such as odor and growth. For that reason, many carpet jobs keep the carpet and replace the pad.

Pad from a clean-water loss caught within a short time is sometimes dried instead of replaced, but a crew confirms it with moisture readings and not by touch.

Drying carpet in place, floating it or taking it out

Crews choose among three approaches. Which one fits depends on the water, the carpet and the room.

  1. Dry in place. Extract the water, then use air movers and a dehumidifier while the carpet stays tacked down. This fits light, clean-water losses.
  2. Float the carpet. Release it from the tack strip along one wall, lift it so air reaches the pad and subfloor and dry it. Pad is replaced, and the carpet is stretched back afterward.
  3. Remove and replace. Take out carpet and pad, dry and clean the subfloor and install new material. This is the path for contaminated water or carpet that has delaminated.

The drying equipment and the daily readings are the same as described on the water mitigation page, and removal of soaked material is covered under water damage cleanup.

Berber, cut pile and the backing underneath

Loop carpet such as Berber can snag and run when it is lifted and stretched, which makes floating riskier. Cut pile is more forgiving. Synthetic fibers tolerate water well, but the jute or latex backing under them can separate or smell when it stays wet.

A crew checks the backing, the seams and the tack strip. Wet wood tack strip can stain or swell, and it is usually replaced along with the pad.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

Stay out of any room where water has reached an outlet, a power strip or a lamp cord. Shut off the water at the source and the power to the area only if you can do so dry. Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks and remove loose items. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet, because of the shock risk.

Emergency or can it wait until morning?

Water that is still spreading or has touched wiring should be handled right away. A small, clean spill on a hard-backed area rug can wait for a morning call as long as the source is stopped. The longer carpet and pad stay wet, the more likely they are to need replacing and the more likely odor is to set in.

Odors and mold prevention

A sour smell from damp carpet means water is still trapped. Mold that follows a covered loss can be part of the claim, according to the state insurance department, which also cautions that slow cleanup is how mold takes hold. Fast drying is the best prevention.

If the carpet has been wet for several days or you smell mildew, tell dispatch. A crew may check the subfloor and the wall base before putting anything back, and may recommend mold remediation if growth is present.

What affects the cost of carpet water damage restoration

Carpet work is priced from the room and the materials. A crew explains the scope once the readings are taken.

  • Water categoryContaminated water means removal, where clean water may allow drying in place.
  • Square footageMore area means more extraction time, equipment and material.
  • Pad and tack stripPad replacement and new tack strip add materials and labor.
  • Carpet type and ageOlder or looped carpet is harder to float and restretch.
  • Subfloor conditionWet or stained subfloor adds drying and cleaning steps.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Describe the room, the water and the carpet type when you call.

Insurance, flood and who to call about the water

The California Department of Insurance Flood Facts page says standard homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, and that flood coverage is sold separately through the National Flood Insurance Program. Carpet soaked by a pipe or an appliance is a different question from carpet soaked by surface water, so tell your insurer exactly where the water came from.

If the water is coming from the street side or the meter, LADWP lists 1-800-DIAL-DWP as its customer contact line. Keep photos, receipts and the old carpet sample until your adjuster says it can go.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving wet pad in place under dry-looking carpet.
  • Covering damp carpet with plastic, which traps moisture.
  • Stretching carpet back before the subfloor is dry.
  • Using a space heater to speed drying, which dries the surface and leaves the pad wet.
  • Discarding the carpet before photographing it for your insurer.
