Emergency Storm Water Damage Cleanup in Tarzana, CA

Storms push water into a house in ways that are not a flood and not a roof leak. Dispatch is answered all day and all night.

Storm water damage cleanup covers the harm done when a winter storm sends water into a house through paths other than a failed roof or rising flood water. Rain blown against a window wall, a patio drain that cannot keep up and mud that slides to a garage door are all examples.

Our line is answered 24/7. A crew pulls out the water and debris, removes materials that cannot be saved and dries the rest. We do not clear storm drains or fix grading, and we tell you which trade handles that part.

How this differs from a flood or a roof leak

The three situations overlap, so it helps to separate them. Flood water rises from the ground or a channel and is usually treated as contaminated. Roof leak damage starts above and works down, and it is covered on the roof leak water damage page.

Storm water damage sits between them. The water enters sideways or from a failed drain, often in moderate amounts, but it carries mud and yard debris. If water has risen across a floor from outside, flood damage restoration is the better fit.

Where storm water gets into Tarzana homes

Entry points are usually small and repeat from one storm to the next. Knowing them helps a crew find the wet materials quickly.

  • Sliding doors and windows on the side facing the wind, where rain is driven under the frame.
  • Garage doors that lack a tight bottom seal, especially where the driveway slopes toward them.
  • Patio and courtyard drains that clog with leaves and send water against the wall.
  • Downspouts that empty beside the foundation instead of away from it.
  • Planters and raised beds that hold water against stucco.

The Los Angeles County Office of Emergency Management storm-season guide advises residents to clear outdoor drains and gutters and to use sandbags to direct runoff and protect structures. Those steps work best before the rain starts.

Heavy rain in the Valley

The Valley can receive a great deal of rain in a short time. A National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Weather Prediction Center summary of the February 4 to 6, 2024 storm lists Woodland Hills at about 11 inches and Downtown Los Angeles at about 8.5 inches.

Storms like that fill drains faster than they empty, and the overflow finds the lowest door or window. A house with no history of leaks can show wet baseboards and soaked carpet after one such event.

Mud and debris on the hillsides south of Ventura Boulevard

Tarzana is mostly valley floor, but the streets near the Santa Monica Mountains sit below slopes. The Los Angeles County storm guide warns that winter storms bring danger of mud and debris flows in steep hillside areas.

A United States Geological Survey study of the January 1969 storms in the Santa Monica Mountains found that debris flows were triggered when rainfall intensity exceeded about 0.20 to 0.25 inch per hour. That study is old, but it explains why hillside homes watch the rain rate and not only the total.

Los Angeles County Public Works describes cleaning out debris basins so they keep the capacity to protect downstream residents. A clear basin helps the street, and it does not replace keeping your own drains open. Homes on those streets are described on the Tarzana Hills page.

First-hour safety steps

Storm conditions add risks that a plumbing leak does not have. Keep these in mind before you start moving things.

  1. Stay out of any room with standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in the water.
  2. Do not walk through muddy water in bare feet, and wear boots and gloves if you must.
  3. If a downed power line or tree is involved, stay away and call 911 and the utility.
  4. Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  5. Photograph the water line, the entry point and the damaged items, then call the line.

Mud-laden water should be treated as contaminated

Water that has crossed a yard, a street or a planter can carry soil, fertilizer and animal waste. Keep children and pets out, wash hands after contact and let a crew decide which materials can be cleaned in place.

Emergency cleanup: what the crew does right now

A storm call is treated as urgent when water is still entering, when it covers electrical equipment or when mud is inside the house. The crew works in the order below.

  1. Block or divert the entry point where possible and note it for the later repair.
  2. Pump or extract standing water and shovel or vacuum out mud and debris.
  3. Remove wet carpet pad, baseboard and the lower section of drywall if the water was muddy.
  4. Clean and sanitize hard surfaces and framing, then set air movers and dehumidifiers.
  5. Record moisture readings daily until the structure is dry.

ANSI/IICRC S500 describes how water categories and drying classes guide this work.

What affects the price of storm water cleanup

Storm jobs vary more than most. The factors below shape the scope once a crew has seen the house.

  • Mud and debris volumeHauling and cleaning silt takes time and adds disposal steps.
  • Water categoryMuddy water usually calls for more removal than clean water.
  • Rooms and flooringCarpet, wood and tile respond differently and some cannot be saved.
  • Entry pointA single door is simpler than water that came in at several places.
  • Rebuild scopeDrying and cleanup are separate from repairs, paint and flooring.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Call and describe the storm and the rooms affected.

Insurance and storm water

Insurance regulators in California treat flood as its own category with its own policy, apart from ordinary homeowners water damage coverage. Whether storm water counts as flood on your policy depends on how it entered, so describe it exactly to your insurer.

The department also advises keeping receipts for temporary repairs and not discarding damaged property until the adjuster says so. You can look up your flood zone on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Prevention before the next storm

Clear drains and gutters before rain, point downspouts away from the house and check door seals. These steps cost little and stop most of the repeat calls we hear about.
