Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Tarzana, CA

Drying is a measured process with a finish line. This page explains the equipment, the readings and how a crew decides when the structure is dry.

Call (818) 337-2642

Structural drying is the part of a water loss that happens after the standing water is gone. It removes the moisture that soaked into framing, subfloors, drywall, plaster and concrete, which no mop or wet vacuum can reach. Done well, it saves materials that would otherwise be torn out.

It sits in the middle of the usual sequence. Extraction and emergency steps come first, and they are described on the water mitigation page. Removal of ruined materials and sanitizing follow, and they are covered on the water damage cleanup page. This page is about the drying itself.

How water leaves a building

Wet materials dry by evaporation. Water in a stud or a subfloor turns to vapor, moves into the surrounding air and has to be carried away. The pace depends on three things: how warm the material is, how much air moves across it and how much moisture the air already holds.

That last point is the heart of psychrometrics, the study of air and moisture. Air can only hold so much vapor. When the room air is already damp, wet materials give up water slowly, so crews lower the humidity of the room instead of only blowing air around.

Why dry Valley air does not dry a sealed house

Tarzana summers are hot and dry. The National Weather Service reading of 121 degrees Fahrenheit at the official Woodland Hills site at Pierce College on September 6, 2020 is the kind of heat people point to when they assume a wet house will dry on its own.

It will not, at least not safely. Outdoor air only helps when it moves through a space on purpose. In a closed house, vapor leaving wet materials stays inside, and room humidity climbs. Opening windows does not control where that air goes.

Local history accounts say many Reseda houses date to the 1940s and 1950s. Layered finishes in older homes trap moisture, so a surface that feels dry can sit on a wet core.

Safety around drying equipment

Drying equipment runs for days, so a few habits keep the house and the people in it safe.

  • Keep cords out of standing water and do not stack extension cords or run them under rugs.
  • Do not unplug equipment to charge a phone or run an appliance, because shared circuits can overload.
  • If a breaker trips or you smell something hot, turn the machine off and call dispatch.

The equipment and what each piece does

A drying setup is a small system, not a pile of fans. Each part has a job, and the crew places them according to what the readings show.

Air movers

Air movers push air in a skimming pattern across wet surfaces so vapor is lifted off them. Placement matters more than quantity. They are set along walls and across floors, not aimed at one spot.

Dehumidifiers

Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers work like a large cold coil and suit most homes. Desiccant units use a drying wheel and are chosen for cooler spaces or very low humidity targets. Both pull vapor out of the air so it does not settle back into the framing.

Meters and cameras

Pin and pinless meters read moisture in wood and drywall, and a thermal camera shows cool patches that suggest wet material. A crew compares all three.

Drying chambers, wall cavities and floors

Crews close off the wet area with plastic sheeting to make a drying chamber, which is easier to dehumidify than the whole house.

Wall cavities can be dried by opening the base of the wall or by injecting air through small holes. Floating floors and some wood floors can be dried with mats that pull moisture through the boards. A crew decides between drying in place and removal based on readings, not on appearance.

Classes, daily logs and the finish line

ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, groups losses into four classes by how much moisture materials absorbed and how quickly they release it. Class 1 is the lightest and Class 4 covers dense materials that dry very slowly. The class guides how much equipment is planned, and more fans without matching dehumidifiers only recirculate vapor.

Each visit adds readings and humidity logs to a record. The finish line is a dry standard, meaning the readings of wet materials match those of similar unaffected materials in the same house. When they do, equipment leaves. A calendar date alone never ends the job.

What affects the cost of structural drying

Drying cost follows equipment and days on site. These are the factors.

  • Materials involvedDense materials such as concrete, plaster and hardwood dry more slowly than carpet or drywall.

Drying records and your insurance claim

The California Department of Insurance residential claims guide says that delay in cleanup can result in the growth of mold, and that mold from a covered cause may be covered under a homeowners policy. It also advises reporting water damage to your agent or company right away.

Moisture logs, photos and equipment records show how long materials were wet and what was done. They do not decide coverage, and no company can promise that a claim will be approved. Ask your insurer what it wants to see before work begins.

Mistakes to avoid

A few habits cause most drying problems. Avoid them and the process goes more smoothly.

  • Closing up walls or laying new flooring before readings reach the dry standard.
  • Turning machines off at night because they are loud, which restarts the clock.
