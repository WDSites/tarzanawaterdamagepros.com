Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Tarzana, CA

A kitchen is a stack of materials that each hold water differently. Restoration dries the stack, from the floor covering down to the subfloor.

Call (818) 337-2642

A kitchen holds more water risk than any other room in the house. It has a sink, a dishwasher, often a refrigerator line, and a floor made of several layers. When something leaks, the water does not stay on the surface. It runs under cabinets, into toe kicks and down into the subfloor.

This page is about the kitchen as a set of building materials. The machines that leak are covered on appliance leak water damage, and standing water that covers the whole floor is handled under emergency water removal.

The kitchen as a stack of materials

Water behaves differently in each layer. Tile and sheet vinyl shed it, but the seams and the underlayment beneath them do not. Laminate and engineered flooring swell at the edges. Hardwood cups. Cabinet boxes made from particleboard swell and lose strength, while solid wood and plywood can often be dried.

Toe kicks and the base of cabinets sit at floor level and wick water upward. Countertops are usually safe, but the sink base and the cabinet floor beneath a sink absorb leaks directly. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the sink cabinet, water often collects where nobody looks.

First-hour safety steps

Kitchens combine water with outlets and gas lines. Work through these steps only as far as is safe.

  1. Close the shutoff valve under the sink or the main valve if the source is unclear.
  2. Stay out of standing water that reaches outlets, a range cord or the dishwasher plug.
  3. If you cook with gas and smell it, leave the house and call 911 and the gas utility.
  4. Empty the sink cabinet and move food, pans and small appliances to a dry place.
  5. Photograph the floor, the cabinets and the source, then call dispatch.

Our process for a wet kitchen

Crews extract standing water and then take moisture readings of the floor, the cabinet bases and the wall behind them. A thermal camera finds cool, wet areas under finished surfaces. Toe kicks are removed to let air reach the cabinet base, and the dishwasher or sink cavity is opened when readings call for it.

Air movers push air along the floor and into cabinet bases, while a low-grain refrigerant dehumidifier removes moisture from the room. Some floors and boxes can be dried in place. Others, such as swollen particleboard and laminate, are removed so the subfloor can dry. Crews return to read moisture until materials meet the dry standard in ANSI/IICRC S500.

Permits, licenses and who does the rebuild

Restoration dries the kitchen. Rebuilding cabinets, flooring or plumbing is a separate job. The city building department's guidance covers single-family repair work and lists plumbing, electrical and heating permits as their own items. It also says to call for inspection before covering any work.

Rebuild work above the state contractor board's dollar threshold needs a licensed contractor, so look up whoever you hire, and ask what permits they will pull.

What sets the price of kitchen water damage restoration

Kitchens vary more than most rooms. These are the factors a crew weighs.

  • Flooring typeTile, vinyl, laminate and hardwood each dry and fail differently.
  • Cabinet constructionParticleboard boxes often come out, while plywood and solid wood can be dried.
  • Size of the wet areaA sink base is a smaller job than a floor that reached the dining area.
  • Subfloor involvementWet subfloor adds drying days or removal.
  • Water categoryClean supply water is simpler than a dishwasher drain or sewage backup.
  • Rebuild scopeReplacing cabinets and floors is separate from drying.

Insurance and mold in a kitchen loss

The state insurance department notes that mold from a covered cause can fall under a homeowners policy and that waiting to clean up lets mold grow. It also advises reporting water damage to your agent or company right away. Ask your insurer what applies to your policy and keep every receipt.

Moisture inside a cabinet base or wall can grow mold quickly if it is left closed. If you smell mustiness after a leak, see mold remediation.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Laying new flooring over a subfloor that was never read with a meter.
  • Pulling toe kicks without a plan to dry the cabinet base.
  • Closing a wet cabinet with a fresh coat of paint.
  • Leaving a dishwasher running while the floor is wet.
  • Assuming the leak is fixed because the puddle is gone.
