Where Melody Acres sits in Tarzana

The City of Los Angeles Encino-Tarzana Community Plan names Melody Acres as a residential area north of Ventura Boulevard. The plan text places it between Tampa Avenue on the east and Corbin Avenue on the west, and between Topham Street on the north and Martha Street on the south.

The same plan describes the area as having large estate lots, quaint narrow streets and a distinct mix of housing styles. We treat those boundaries as approximate. Neighbors may draw the edges slightly differently, so check your own address and street name when you call.

What large lots and narrow streets mean for a water loss

A large lot is not the same as a large house, so the plan's wording says nothing about floor area. As a general plumbing point, larger lots often have longer irrigation lines, outdoor spigots and detached structures such as guest houses or pool equipment rooms. Each one is another place a leak can start.

A mixed housing stock also means no single drying plan fits every home. A slab floor, a raised floor over a crawl space and a second story each hold water differently, so the crew should check the structure before choosing equipment.

Narrow streets matter for logistics. Extraction units, hoses and dehumidifiers have to come in from the curb, so mention parking limits, gates or a steep or narrow driveway when you call. That one detail helps the crew plan the carry from the street to the wet room.

Safety first: the first hour in Melody Acres

Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely. It is usually near where the supply line enters the house. Do not walk through standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it, and keep children and pets out of the room.

Do not switch breakers on while standing in water. If you smell gas, see sparking or hear buzzing from wet wiring, leave the house and call 911. Photograph the damage from a dry spot before you move anything, then lift small valuables out of the water.

How extraction and drying work

Extraction pulls standing water out with truck-mounted or portable units. Then a crew measures wall, floor and framing moisture with meters and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. The goal is to bring materials back to a normal moisture level, and readings during the job show whether that is happening.

Some materials can be dried in place and others cannot. Wet insulation, soaked carpet pad and drywall that has softened are often removed. Hardwood and plaster depend on how long they stayed wet. A crew should explain which items stay and why before cutting anything out.

Sewer lines and lateral responsibility

If the water is coming up through a drain, treat it as contaminated and stay out. According to LA Sanitation, the city sewer agency, the owner maintains the full lateral pipe between the building and the middle of the street. A backup that begins in that pipe is typically the owner's repair.

Our sewage backup cleanup page explains the cleanup side. Whether the blockage is in the lateral or the city main is a question for a plumber and the sewer agency, and it affects who pays for the repair.

Insurance documentation after a leak

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of your policy, so read the policy and ask your insurer. The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners to keep receipts, keep a log of calls, take reasonable steps to protect the property and avoid discarding damaged items until the adjuster says to.

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope of work can help describe what happened and what was done. None of that guarantees a claim will be approved, and we do not decide coverage. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, which is sold separately.
