Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Tarzana, CA

Sewage is contaminated water, and the cleanup is about containment and protection before anything is dried.

Call (818) 337-2642

A sewage backup is wastewater coming back up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or shower instead of leaving the house. In Tarzana it usually starts with a blockage or break somewhere between your plumbing and the main in the street. The water that reaches your floor is contaminated, and under ANSI/IICRC S500 that makes it Category 3, the most hazardous tier.

This page covers the first hour, who owns which pipe, what a crew removes and disinfects, and the mistakes that make a backup worse. The line is answered 24/7, and a same-day visit depends on which crews are free.

Emergency sewage backup: when to call right now

Call right now if sewage is rising in a tub or floor drain, if it has reached carpet or drywall, or if more than one fixture is backing up at the same time. Several fixtures failing together usually points to a blockage low in the line and not a single clogged toilet.

Dispatch asks which fixtures are involved, how far the water has spread and whether anyone is in the room. Those answers decide the protective gear and equipment that go on the truck. Arrival time is not guaranteed, so ask what is realistic for your address.

First-hour safety steps

Sewage carries bacteria and other organisms, so the first hour is about protecting people. These steps assume the room is safe to enter and that no outlet or cord is sitting in the water.

  1. Stop using water in the house. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or washer or take a shower, because every gallon sent down the drain can come back up.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the affected rooms and off wet floors.
  3. If water has reached an outlet, a panel or an appliance, stay out of the room and call 911 and the utility before you call us. Never stand in water to operate a breaker.
  4. Wear waterproof boots and rubber gloves if you must cross the area, and wash your hands with soap and clean water afterward.
  5. Leave household fans and shop vacuums alone. They spread contaminated droplets and are hard to clean afterward.
  6. Photograph the water line and the fixtures involved before anything is moved.

Do not clean up sewage yourself

Health questions about exposure belong with your physician, not with this page. For the house, the plan is to contain, remove and disinfect, which takes protective gear and equipment most homes do not have.

Who owns what: the city main and your lateral

In the City of Los Angeles the public sewer main under the street belongs to the city. LA Sanitation, the city department that runs it, says on its program page that it operates over 6,700 miles of public sewer pipeline. Your house connects to that main through a private pipe called a lateral.

The same LA Sanitation page says property owners are responsible for maintaining and repairing their entire lateral, which runs from the building to the middle of the street. That includes the stretch under the sidewalk and the street edge, which surprises many owners.

LA Sanitation lists a 24-hour Customer Care Center at 1-800-773-2489, and its program page ties that number to reporting public sewer odors. If you smell sewage outside near a manhole, that is the city's line. For the cleanup inside your house, call us.

LA Sanitation's lateral repair rebate page adds that after a backup the owner will need to find the cause and may need the lateral repaired. A plumber finds and fixes the cause. We handle what the water touched.

How backups start

Roots and grease do most of the damage. LA Sanitation's sewer FAQ says that over 50 percent of sewer backups are caused by tree roots and poured fats, oils and grease. Roots find the joints in older pipe and grow into them, and grease cools and coats the pipe wall until the opening shrinks.

Those are plumbing causes, and restoration does not remove them. Cleanup without a line repair invites a second backup, so the plumber visit matters as much as ours.

The same FAQ recommends a backwater valve when the lowest fixture in a building sits below the street manhole cover, installed by a licensed plumber with an LADBS permit. A house on a slope below the street can ask a plumber whether that applies.

How we clean up after a sewage backup

The order matters, because contamination spreads on boots, on air and on wet materials that stay in place. A typical visit runs like this.

  1. Seal off the area with plastic sheeting, and where the job calls for it run negative air machines with HEPA filtration so contaminated air does not drift into clean rooms.
  2. Put on protective gear, including waterproof suits, gloves, boots and respirators chosen for the job.
  3. Extract the liquid with pumps and extraction units, then remove porous materials that soaked it up, such as carpet, pad, baseboard and the lower section of drywall.
  4. Clean and disinfect hard surfaces and framing, and let the product stay wet for the contact time on its label.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, record moisture readings daily and clear the area before any rebuild.

The drying stage follows the same principles described on the water mitigation page. The difference is that contaminated materials come out first.

What sets the price of a sewage backup cleanup

Cleanup is priced from what the water reached and how much has to be removed, which is why a phone number is never exact. These are the factors a crew weighs once the room is assessed.

  • Area and depthThe number of rooms and how far the water traveled set the extraction and removal time.
  • Materials affectedCarpet, pad, drywall and cabinets that must be removed add labor and disposal.
  • Protective setupContainment, negative air and protective gear add work that clean-water jobs do not need.
  • Time since the backupWater that has sat for days spreads further and soaks deeper.
  • Drying and rebuildDrying days are separate from the rebuild, which is a later scope.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Ask the crew to list what will be removed, cleaned and dried so you can compare it with any other offer.

Insurance and sewage backups

Whether a policy responds to water that backs up through drains is a question of policy wording, and some policies handle it separately from other water damage. Ask your insurer which section applies before you assume either way.

The California Department of Insurance advises reporting water damage to your agent or company right away and notes that a delay in cleanup can allow mold to grow. Keep photos, receipts and the plumber's invoice, and do not discard damaged items until the adjuster says you can.

Mistakes to avoid

Each of these either spreads contamination or leaves the cause in place.

  • Running water to see whether the drain has cleared.
  • Mopping or wet-vacuuming sewage with household tools.
  • Waiting a day for the carpet to dry before calling.
  • Skipping the plumber, so the same blockage returns.
  • Throwing everything away before photographing it for your adjuster.
