A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs through or beneath the concrete floor of a house. The pipe is out of sight, so the first sign is often damage to the flooring above it, not a drip. Many houses in the Valley sit on slabs, which makes this a real possibility here.

Our crews do not repair the pipe. A plumber finds and fixes the leak, and we dry and clean what the water reached. Call the line and tell dispatch what you see, and we can explain the sequence for your house.

How a slab leak develops

Water lines under a slab are surrounded by concrete and soil. Over many years, a copper line can wear at a bend, a joint can fail or soil movement can strain a fitting. The leak may be a pinhole that runs for months before anyone notices.

Older houses are more likely to have pipes that have reached the end of their service life. Reseda, for example, shifted from orchards to single-family housing in the mid to late 1940s according to local histories, and original plumbing from that era may still be in use in places.

Earthquakes can add strain. The January 17, 1994 Northridge earthquake broke water and gas lines across the area, so a home repaired after that quake may have patched lines in walls or slabs. If you buy an older house, ask for plumbing repair records.

A simple meter test you can do first

A meter test is the quick check here: with every fixture off, watch whether the gauge on the utility meter still turns. LADWP describes the test, and it takes a few minutes.

Turn off every faucet and appliance, including the ice maker and the irrigation system, then watch the gauge. If it keeps moving, the leak is somewhere in your lines. If the gauge is still, the problem may be inside the house or elsewhere.

First-hour safety steps

If you suspect a slab leak, take a few precautions before the plumber and the crew arrive.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, usually found near where the supply line enters the house.
  2. Turn off the water heater if hot water is involved, so it does not heat an empty tank.
  3. Keep power away from wet floors and do not use extension cords on damp surfaces.
  4. Do not drill or cut the slab yourself.
  5. Note the date and what you saw, then call the line and a plumber.

A warm spot on the floor can be hot

A hot water line leaking under a slab can warm the surface and scald bare skin. Keep children and pets off the area until a plumber has isolated the line.

Urgent: when a slab leak cannot wait

A slab leak becomes an emergency when water is visible on the floor, when it is spreading to carpet or wood or when the meter is spinning fast. Water pooling above or around the slab soaks flooring and baseboards within hours.

A slow leak with no visible water is still worth a same-day call to a plumber, and a call to us if flooring has started to change.

What we do after the leak is stopped

Once the plumber has isolated or repaired the line, our work starts. Concrete holds water, so the goal is to dry the floor covering, the subfloor and the base of the walls. The reference we measure against is ANSI/IICRC S500, which describes drying goals but is not a certification we claim.

  1. Map moisture across the floor and up the walls with meters and a thermal camera.
  2. Lift wet carpet, pad or floating flooring that cannot be dried in place.
  3. Extract any standing water and set air movers to cross the surface.
  4. Run dehumidifiers until the slab and the wall bases reach their dry standard.
  5. Take readings on return visits before equipment is removed.

Concrete dries slowly, so patience is part of the job. Carpet that was soaked is covered on the carpet water damage restoration page.

What affects the price of slab leak cleanup

Slab leak cleanup varies with how long the water has been rising and what is on the floor.

  • Flooring typeCarpet, wood, laminate and tile dry or fail in different ways.
  • Area wetMore floor and baseboard means more equipment and more days.
  • Duration of the leakMonths of slow seepage reach more materials than a single event.
  • Drying time for concreteSlabs hold moisture, so drying can run longer than on a wood floor.
  • Rebuild scopeFlooring and trim replacement are separate from drying.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Call and describe where the floor is wet.

Insurance and a slab leak

The utility's own guidance puts repair of leaks past the meter on the property owner, which includes a slab leak. Your insurer may treat plumbing leaks differently from other losses, so read the policy and ask.

The California Department of Insurance advises keeping receipts and not discarding damaged items until the adjuster says so. Photograph the floor before anything is lifted.
