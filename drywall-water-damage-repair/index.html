Drywall Water Damage Repair in Tarzana, CA

Walls hide the worst of a leak low in the cavity, so repair starts with readings and ends with a finish that matches.

Call (818) 337-2642

Drywall is gypsum between sheets of paper, and both parts take on water quickly. When a wall gets wet, the paper bubbles, the gypsum softens and the board starts to lose strength. Walls are also where plumbing runs, so a leak inside a cavity can soak the board from behind long before a stain shows.

This page covers walls. Ceilings follow the same repair path with a few extra steps and are covered on the water damage repair page. The sections below explain how a crew reads a wall, how much comes out and what a good finish looks like.

Reading a wall before touching it

The stain you see is rarely the full story. Water runs down inside a wall cavity, wicks up the paper and pools at the bottom plate, so the worst damage is often lower or higher than the visible mark.

  • Paint that bubbles or peels points to water behind the surface.
  • A soft spot that gives under light pressure means the gypsum has failed.
  • A stain with a brown edge suggests water arrived more than once.
  • A cool, clammy wall can mean moisture is still present.
  • Swelling at the baseboard shows water has reached the bottom of the wall.

Moisture mapping and the first cut

A crew uses a moisture meter and, where needed, a thermal camera to outline wet areas, then compares readings with a dry wall in the same house, in the way ANSI/IICRC S500 describes moisture mapping. That map decides where to cut.

The usual cut is a flood cut, a straight line through the drywall a couple of feet above the highest wet reading. Some jobs call for two feet and others for four feet, depending on how high the water wicked. Cutting above the wet zone lets the cavity breathe and exposes insulation and framing so they can dry.

First-hour safety steps for a wet wall

If a wall is wet around outlets or switches, stay away and cut power to that circuit only if you can do so from a dry panel. Do not poke a bulging wall near a light fixture. Shut off the water to the room if a pipe is leaking. Wear gloves and eye protection if you open anything, because wet drywall is heavy and crumbles.

Emergency or can it wait?

A wall that is actively wet, sagging or near wiring should be handled the same day. A dry stain from an old leak can wait for a scheduled visit, but the cause should be found first. Painting over it hides the problem and often brings it back.

What comes out and what stays

Not every wet wall is replaced. The choice depends on how long the board was wet, what the water carried and what is behind it.

How the wall is rebuilt

Once readings show the cavity is dry, rebuilding follows a fixed order. Skipping drying is the most common way a good-looking repair fails.

  1. Verify that framing and remaining drywall read dry against the dry reference wall.
  2. Replace insulation and hang new drywall, using moisture-resistant board where the room calls for it.
  3. Tape and mud the joints, let each coat dry and sand it smooth.
  4. Match the texture, since orange peel, knockdown and smooth walls each need a different method.
  5. Prime, paint the full wall to the nearest break and reinstall baseboard and trim.

A patch that looks perfect under primer can show once painted, so ask how the finish is blended into the old wall. Paint matching depends on the age and sheen of the existing coat.

Permits and licensing questions

The city's building department publishes a homeowner's permit guide that treats single-family repair and remodel work as permit territory, with separate plumbing, electrical and heating permits where those systems are touched, and it says to call for inspection before covering any work. Whether a particular drywall repair needs a permit is not something we can state, so ask LADBS about your address.

California requires a contractor license once a job passes the state's dollar threshold and for work that needs a building permit. Check any contractor's license on the CSLB site before work begins.

Insurance and wet drywall

Walls that stay wet invite mold, and the state insurance department notes that mold from a covered cause may be covered while delay makes it worse. Report water damage to your agent early, photograph the wall before anything is cut and keep the removed board until the adjuster says it can be discarded.

A mitigation crew can give you moisture readings and photos that show how wet the wall was. Ask your insurer what it needs. The repair stage usually follows water mitigation and water damage cleanup.

Mistakes to avoid

Each of these tends to bring the stain back, or lets mold grow inside a sealed wall. If you smell something musty after the repair, see mold remediation.

  • Painting over a stain before the source is fixed.
  • Closing the wall before the cavity reads dry.
  • Patching only the visible spot without checking higher and lower.
  • Leaving wet insulation in place.
  • Skipping a photo record before cutting.
