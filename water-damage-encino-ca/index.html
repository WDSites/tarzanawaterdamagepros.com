When water is spreading in Encino

Water that is still moving through a home is an emergency whether it started at a hillside hose bib or a second-floor bathroom. The first goal is to stop the source and keep the water from reaching more rooms.

Encino homes range from single-story ranch houses on the flats to multi-level homes on the hills. Water in a multi-level house travels down through floor cavities and can appear far from where it started, so a ceiling stain on the lower floor often means the real source is above it.

Hills, basin and boulevard: where Encino's water calls begin

Encino sits on the north slope of the Santa Monica Mountains and is bordered on the north by the Sepulveda Basin. The Sepulveda Dam Recreation Area inside Encino is a flood control basin, completed in 1941 by the Army Corps of Engineers, built to hold back winter flood water along the Los Angeles River.

For a homeowner that geography splits into two kinds of risk. Homes on the hills see runoff from slopes above and plumbing failures from the long supply runs that steep lots need. Homes on the flats see standing water collect in garages and low rooms. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center instead of assuming the neighborhood answer applies to your lot.

A spring, an oak and what they say about Encino weather

Los Encinos State Historic Park, a five-acre state park in Encino, preserves the de la Ossa Adobe, a pond and a natural spring. Water has surfaced here for a long time, so a damp lower level or retaining wall in Encino is not always a plumbing leak. A crew can use moisture readings to tell a leak from groundwater seepage and recommend the right trade.

The park's namesake tree tells a weather story too. The Encino Oak at Ventura Boulevard and Louise Avenue was felled by an El Niño storm on February 7, 1998. El Niño winters bring heavy rain and wind together, which means roof damage and water intrusion together. If a storm tears shingles or drops a limb on your roof, call before the next rain.

Higher-value homes and finishes worth protecting

Census survey data for ZIP 91436 lists median home values above two million dollars. Many Encino homes have hardwood floors, stone, built-in cabinetry and custom millwork, and those finishes are costly to replace and sometimes impossible to match.

That raises the value of fast drying and careful moisture mapping before anything is torn out. Ask the crew to explain which finishes can be dried in place and which cannot, and keep every photo and reading for your insurer. See water mitigation for the drying process.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading in Encino

Stop the source

Close the main water valve. If the source is a hose bib or irrigation line outside, close that branch valve too.

Power and safety

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. For sparking or a gas smell, leave and call 911.

Lift and protect

Move rugs, books and electronics off wet floors and put foil or blocks under furniture legs.

Stay out of dirty water

If water came from a drain or the street, keep people and pets out and do not wade through it.

Call the 24/7 line

Call the line, give your cross streets and describe what is wet.
