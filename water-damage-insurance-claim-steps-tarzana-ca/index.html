Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Tarzana Homeowners

By the Tarzana Water Damage Pros team, 2026

A water damage insurance claim goes best when you protect the home, document everything and talk to your insurer early. These steps follow guidance published by the California Department of Insurance in its Residential Property Claims Guide, adapted to a Tarzana homeowner's first week. They describe a process, and your own policy decides what is covered.

Step 1: make it safe and stop the water

Shut off the main water valve, avoid wet outlets and keep people and pets away from contaminated water. If the loss is active, call the 24/7 line at the number on this page for emergency water removal. Do not wait for the claim to be opened before you protect the property.

Step 2: call your insurer and ask what it needs

Report the loss as soon as you can and ask what documentation the company wants. California regulations set time limits that insurers must meet to acknowledge, evaluate and pay claims, and the Department of Insurance's help line is 1-800-927-4357 if a claim stalls.

Ask about your deductible, whether the adjuster will visit before permanent repairs and whether the policy has limits for water damage or mold. Write down the date, the representative's name and what you were told.

Step 3: document the damage

The Department of Insurance advises keeping a log of phone calls and copies of correspondence, listing damaged items with brand names and model numbers and photographing the damage. Keep old receipts and bills that show what items were worth, and do not discard damaged items until the adjuster says so.

A restoration crew adds moisture readings, thermal photos and an equipment log. Those show what was wet and for how long, and that record is useful to an adjuster who arrives after drying has begun.

Step 4: protect the property from further damage

The Department of Insurance says policyholders should take reasonable steps to secure the property, and that insurers reimburse reasonable protection costs when you keep receipts. It also advises against extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster has assessed the damage. Emergency drying is protection, while a full remodel is a permanent repair.

Step 5: get written bids from licensed contractors

Get bids that list prices line by line, as the Department of Insurance recommends. California requires a contractor license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, a threshold that rose from $500 on January 1, 2025, and the Contractors State License Board lets you check a license number. Compare scopes, not only totals.

What this means for a Tarzana home

The Census-based figure for ZIP 91356 puts median home value at about $1.2 million, so repair scopes often run into finishes such as hardwood, stone and custom cabinetry. A detailed bid and a clear moisture record carry real weight in that situation. Ask the crew to explain which materials can be dried in place and which must be replaced.

Tarzana lies within Los Angeles County and the City of Los Angeles, so ask the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety whether the repairs need permits. Homes in Encino and Woodland Hills follow the same rules.

Keep a claim file from day one

A tidy record saves time later. Create a folder on your phone or computer and put everything in it as it happens. Adjusters ask for the same items in the same order, so having them ready speeds the process.

  • Photos and short videos of every affected room before cleanup.
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate age and value.
  • Receipts for emergency repairs, hotel stays and equipment.
  • Names, dates and claim numbers from every call.
  • Copies of the policy declarations page.

What the adjuster usually looks at

The adjuster tries to understand the cause, the extent and the cost to repair. They may take moisture readings, inspect the source and ask for estimates. Being present, with your notes ready, helps you answer questions clearly.

If you disagree with the findings, ask for the reasoning in writing and consider getting a second estimate. Policies describe a process for disputes, and your insurer can point you to it.

How drying records fit in

A restoration crew documents moisture readings, equipment placed and materials removed. These records show that you took reasonable steps to limit the damage, and many adjusters ask for them.

Emergency drying under water mitigation often starts before the claim is approved, because delay makes the loss worse. The repair stage follows under water damage repair once the structure is dry and the scope is agreed.

What claims do not promise

No crew, and no website, can promise that a claim will be approved or paid. The insurer makes those decisions under your policy. A restoration company can help you protect the home and record what happened, and water damage repair can follow once the adjuster has done their work.
