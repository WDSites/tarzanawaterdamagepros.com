Orchards to tract homes: how Reseda's housing was built

Reseda was founded in 1912 as the town of Marian and renamed Reseda around 1921 or 1922. For decades it stayed largely agricultural. In the mid to late 1940s orchards were replaced with single-family houses, and by the early 1950s the area had become one of the Valley's most populated communities.

That building period matters for water damage. Houses from the 1940s and 1950s can still carry original plumbing in places, including galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, and older lines are more likely to fail at joints. If you are not sure what your pipes are made of, ask a plumber to look before a leak makes the decision for you.

When water is spreading in Reseda

Old plumbing fails quietly and then loudly. A pinhole that has been wetting the inside of a wall for months can let go all at once, and the first sign may be a soft patch of drywall or a spreading stain on the other side of a room.

If you see a stain, hear water behind a wall or find a section of floor that feels warm or spongy, treat it as urgent. Drying standards such as ANSI/IICRC S500 guide the work that follows. Shut off the main valve, then call. Water mitigation starts with finding where the moisture actually is.

The earthquake that started near Reseda Boulevard

The Northridge earthquake of January 17, 1994 had its epicenter between Arminta and Ingomar Streets, just west of Reseda Boulevard, even though it carries Northridge's name. Water lines were severed and some streets flooded. Five days after the quake an estimated 40,000 to 60,000 customers still lacked public water service.

For Reseda homeowners the lesson is practical. A house that was shaken in 1994 may have had pipe repairs that nobody remembers. If you are buying or reviewing an older home, ask for any records of plumbing work.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading in Reseda

Stop the source

Shut the main valve, usually where the supply pipe enters the house. In an apartment, ask the manager for the building shutoff.

Power and safety

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. If you see sparks or smell gas, leave the house and call 911.

Lift and protect

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. Put blocks or foil under furniture legs.

Stay out of dirty water

Keep children and pets out of water from a drain or a toilet.

Call the 24/7 line

Call the line and give your address, floor and the source if you know it.
