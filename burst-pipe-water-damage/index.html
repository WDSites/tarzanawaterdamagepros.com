A burst pipe in a Tarzana house is a race between the water and the clock. Supply lines run at household pressure, so a split in a wall or ceiling can empty into the structure for as long as the valve stays open. Close the main, then call. Our line is answered 24/7, including holidays.

This page covers pipe failures that send clean supply water into living space. Water heater tanks, appliance hoses and leaks under a slab have pages of their own, and standing floodwater is handled under emergency water removal.

How pipes fail in local houses

Most bursts are not dramatic explosions. A corroded fitting lets go, a compression joint loosens, a copper line wears through where it rubs a framing member, or a plastic connector cracks with age. Older houses may still carry original supply lines that were installed decades ago, and those lines tend to fail at joints and bends.

Pressure matters too. Water that arrives at a house with high static pressure puts steady strain on every fitting, and a pressure regulator that has stopped working can turn a small weakness into a split. A plumber can check pressure at a hose bib with a simple gauge.

First-hour safety steps

The first hour decides how much of the house is affected. Work through these steps in order, and stop at any step that is not safe to do.

  1. Close the main water valve, usually found near where the supply line enters the house, and open one low tap to relieve pressure.
  2. If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out of the room and shut power off only if the breaker panel is dry and easy to reach.
  3. Photograph the source and each wet room before moving belongings, since your insurer will ask for them.
  4. Lift small items, rugs and electronics off wet floors and place foil or blocks under furniture legs.
  5. Call dispatch and describe where the water started and which rooms are wet.

Electricity, ceilings and gas

A ceiling that is bulging with water can fail without warning, so keep people and pets out from under it. Light fixtures holding water are an electrical hazard. For sparking, buzzing or a gas smell, leave the house and call 911 before calling us.

Emergency burst pipe response, right now and for the next few days

Once the source is stopped, the job is to remove the water and then dry what it soaked. Crews use submersible pumps and wand extractors to pull standing water, then lift wet carpet pad that cannot be dried in place. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map the wet areas room by room.

Air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers go in next, sized to the rooms and the materials. Crews return to read moisture daily and remove equipment only when wet materials reach the dry standard. For reference, the written guidance on this sequence is ANSI/IICRC S500.

A pipe burst begins as clean, Category 1 water. That status does not last. As water sits in carpet and wall cavities, it picks up contaminants and the classification can worsen, which is why the first day carries so much weight.

Do not wait for the water to stop on its own

A city water main failure is a different problem. In October 2013 an eight-inch main installed in the 1950s broke on Topeka Drive in Tarzana and left as many as 25 homes without water overnight, according to a CBS Los Angeles report. A break in the street is the utility's job, while a break inside your walls is yours.

The city water utility says private-property leaks are the owner's to fix, though it can help with shutting off water for emergency repairs. That means a pipe inside your property line needs a plumber for the pipe and a restoration crew for the water.

Frozen pipes are rare here, but not impossible

A hard freeze is uncommon in Tarzana, so a frozen pipe is not the usual cause of a burst. In a rare cold snap, the lines most at risk are outdoor hose bibs, irrigation lines and supply pipes in an uninsulated garage or attic. Pipes in a heated living space almost never freeze.

If a line does split after a cold night, the damage looks the same as any other burst and the response is the same. Close the main, call and describe where the pipe runs. Because freezing is so uncommon locally, we handle it here instead of on a separate page.

What sets the price of burst pipe cleanup

The price follows the size of the loss, which is why a phone estimate is never exact. A crew weighs these factors after the assessment.

  • How long the water ranA line that ran for minutes soaks less than one that ran overnight, and the equipment count follows.
  • Rooms and floors reachedWater that traveled through a ceiling into a lower floor means more extraction and more drying.
  • Materials involvedCarpet pad, insulation and drywall are often removed, while hardwood and tile may be saved.
  • Water category at the time of responseClean water caught early is simpler than water that has degraded.
  • Drying daysLonger drying runs mean more equipment days on the invoice.
  • Repair scope afterwardMitigation ends when materials are dry. Rebuilding is a separate scope.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. A burst pipe in a small bathroom is a different job from one that ran through a two-story house, so call and describe what you see.

Insurance and burst pipes

On the insurance side, the state department of insurance separates flood, which standard homeowners policies leave out, from other water damage, which they usually include, and it urges prompt reporting to your agent or company. It also warns that delay in cleanup can result in the growth of mold. A burst supply line is not flood, so ask your insurer how your policy treats it.

Keep photos, the plumber's invoice and any receipts for temporary repairs. Do not discard damaged materials until the adjuster says you can. Coverage is your insurer's decision, and nothing here promises that a claim will be approved.

Mistakes to avoid after a burst pipe

Each of these turns a manageable loss into a bigger one, and most are easy to avoid with a quick call.

  • Waiting to see whether the water stops. It will not, and each hour adds to the drying list.
  • Running a household vacuum on standing water, which is an electrical hazard.
  • Painting over a ceiling stain before the cavity above has been dried and checked.
  • Leaving wet carpet pad in place because the surface feels dry.
  • Hiring someone to patch the pipe without anyone drying the wall it leaked into.
