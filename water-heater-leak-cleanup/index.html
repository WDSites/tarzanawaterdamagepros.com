A leaking water heater can put a lot of water on the floor in a short time, because a tank holds many gallons and keeps refilling from the supply line until the inlet valve is closed. In many Tarzana homes the heater sits in a garage, a hall closet or a laundry room, so the water reaches finished floors and walls quickly. Call the line at any hour.

This page covers cleanup after a tank or its connections leak. The pipes feeding it are covered under burst pipe water damage, and the plumber or installer handles the heater repair or replacement.

Why water heaters leak

Tanks age from the inside. Sediment settles, the lining wears and the steel corrodes until a seam or the base lets go. That kind of failure cannot be patched, and the whole unit is replaced. A leak from a fitting, a drain valve or the temperature and pressure relief line is a different story and is often a simple repair.

The cause matters for cleanup because a slow weep can wet a floor for weeks before anyone sees it, while a tank failure floods a room in minutes. A crew reads moisture to learn which one happened.

First-hour safety steps

A water heater pairs water with gas or electricity, so safety comes before cleanup. Follow these steps only as far as it is safe to do so.

  1. If the heater is gas, turn the gas control to off. If it is electric, switch off its breaker only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  2. Close the cold water inlet valve on the pipe above the heater. This stops the tank from refilling.
  3. Do not stand in water if an outlet, cord or appliance is in it. Wait for the power to be shut off by someone who can do it safely.
  4. Open a nearby faucet to relieve pressure only after the inlet is closed.
  5. Photograph the heater, the label and the wet areas, then call dispatch.

Gas smell or scorched wiring

A gas smell, a hissing sound or scorched wiring means leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility. Do not flip switches, light anything or try to relight the pilot. Restoration begins only after the hazard is cleared.

Our process for a water heater leak, urgent from the first call

Crews extract standing water with pumps and wand units, then move or lift items that cannot stay wet. Baseboards are pulled, and a moisture meter and thermal camera show how far water went into drywall, flooring and the subfloor.

Air movers and a low-grain refrigerant dehumidifier dry the room. In a garage with a concrete slab, water often drains or evaporates quickly, while a closet with a wood subfloor can hold moisture for days. Crews take readings each visit and remove equipment when materials reach the dry standard described in ANSI/IICRC S500.

Bracing, permits and replacement

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or horizontal displacement during earthquake motion. The Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety lists the same expectation in its single-family notes, which say the water heater must be strapped to the wall.

LADBS also says replacing a water heater needs a plumbing permit unless a contractor with a valid LADBS Certificate of Registration does the work, and that route is limited to replacing a unit of equivalent capacity. Ask the installer how permits and inspection will be handled. A restoration crew does not replace the heater.

Insurance and a failed water heater

The state insurance department draws a line between flood and other water damage for homeowners policies. A tank that fails is not a flood, but how your policy treats the heater itself is a question for your insurer. Photograph the label and keep the plumber's invoice.

Keep receipts for temporary repairs and do not throw away the old tank until your adjuster says you can. Coverage is decided by your insurer, and nothing here promises approval.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Mopping up and moving on while the subfloor stays wet.
  • Leaving the cold inlet open and letting the tank keep refilling.
  • Relighting a gas pilot with water on the floor.
  • Skipping the strapping when the new unit is installed.
  • Storing cardboard against a heater, where a slow weep goes unnoticed.
