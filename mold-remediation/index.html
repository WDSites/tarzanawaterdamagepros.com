Mold Remediation in Tarzana, CA

Mold follows moisture. We find the water source, contain the area and remove the growth following published mold remediation practice.

Mold remediation in Tarzana begins with the moisture that fed the growth, and it follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for professional mold remediation. Our line is open 24/7. If you have found mold after a leak, avoid disturbing it, keep the area closed off, and do not run fans that could spread spores through the house.

Most mold calls trace back to a plumbing leak, an air conditioner condensate line, a roof opening or a past flood. Fixing the water source comes first, because remediation without drying only buys time. The drying side is covered on the water mitigation page.

Musty smell, dark spots or a ceiling stain that keeps coming back?

Most people do not search for remediation until something looks or smells wrong. A damp odor in a closet, dark spots on a bathroom ceiling or a wall that never quite dries after a repair are common triggers. Each of these points to moisture that never fully left, and removing the growth while the moisture remains does not last.

Testing and inspection come first when the source is unclear. A trained inspector can use moisture readings, a visual check and, where appropriate, sampling to decide where the problem is and how large it is.

Mold after water damage

Mold needs moisture, organic material and time. EPA guidance on mold, which is widely used in the trade, stresses drying wet materials quickly, often within about a day or two, to prevent growth.

That is why a flooded room or a leaking wall that sat wet for several days is a candidate for inspection even if no mold is visible. Paper-faced drywall, carpet pad and wood framing all provide the organic material mold uses.

Why air conditioning matters in the Valley

Woodland Hills, the next community west of Tarzana, recorded 121 degrees Fahrenheit at Pierce College on September 6, 2020, the highest temperature on record for Los Angeles County according to published accounts. Hot summers mean heavy air conditioning use, and the condensate lines that carry water away from air handlers are a common leak source.

A condensate line that backs up can soak a closet, attic floor or ceiling for weeks before anyone notices. If a ceiling stain appears under an air handler, check the drain line and call before the area begins to smell.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

Remediation is containment, removal and cleaning, in that order. A crew seals the work area with plastic barriers, runs negative air machines with HEPA filtration so air moves into the contained area instead of out of it, and removes or cleans affected materials by type.

Porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet are usually removed when growth is established. Non-porous and semi-porous surfaces can often be cleaned with HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping. Wood framing is cleaned and may be sanded or treated depending on the extent.

The color of mold does not tell you much about how to handle it. A company that calls one color harmless and another dangerous is guessing. This site does not diagnose health effects, and questions about symptoms belong to your physician.

Mold clearance for a home sale or loan

Buyers, lenders and landlords sometimes ask for proof that mold has been addressed. The usual route is independent clearance testing done by a party that is not the remediation company, which avoids a conflict of interest. Ask your lender or agent what documentation they require, because acceptance is their decision and nobody can promise it ahead of time.

Licensing and standards

ANSI/IICRC S520 is a published standard for professional mold remediation, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Ask whether your state licenses mold work and ask for any license numbers the company claims. The Contractors State License Board lets you look up a contractor's license.

Insurance and mold

The California Department of Insurance advises policyholders who suspect mold to contact their adjuster right away and not to attempt cleanup themselves, since cleanup can spread spores. Coverage for mold depends on your policy and the cause, so check your policy and ask your insurer before work begins.

What to do and what not to do while you wait

Small choices before a crew arrives can keep growth from spreading. Follow these steps for safety.

  • Do not disturb visible growth by sanding, scraping or brushing it dry.
  • Do not run a fan directly at the area, because it can spread spores to other rooms.
  • Do not mix cleaning products, since some combinations release harmful fumes.
  • Shut off the water source if a leak is feeding the growth.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the area, and ask a physician about symptoms.
